Workshop roles

Organizers: James White (University of Maryland), Nathan Barrymore (University of Texas at Austin), and Forrest Briscoe (Cornell University).

Speakers: Rocio Bonet (IE Business School), Rem Koning (Harvard Business School), and Seth Carnahan (Washington University in St. Louis).

Brainstorming group leaders: Josh Feng, Geoff Borchhardt, and Reuben Hurst.

Roundtable leaders: Max Kagan, Grady Raines, Beril Yalcinkaya, and Cathy Lu.

Roundtable and brainstorming leaders

Max Kagan, postdoctoral researcher, Columbia Business School (Table 1)

He studies how political views shape workplaces and labor markets. His research links Revelio career histories to voter records, including the VRscores project. This connects directly to the papers at his table, which study how legal and social contestation affects employee entry and exit.

Grady Raines, Cornell University, incoming assistant professor at Indiana University Kelley (Table 2)

He studies entrepreneurship, policy, and inequality using natural experiments and field experiments. The papers at his table evaluate entrepreneurship policy and study entrepreneurial careers.

Beril Yalcinkaya, postdoctoral fellow, Wharton People Analytics (Table 3)

She studies strategic human capital, worker mobility, and how policy changes affect careers, using LinkedIn-based career data. The papers at her table study employee benefits, retention, and gender differences in career advancement.

Cathy Lu, assistant professor, University of Minnesota Carlson School (Table 4)

She studies worker mobility, firm strategy, and stakeholder relationships. The papers at her table study executive mobility and employee reactions to strategic change.

Geoff Borchhardt, assistant professor, University of Oregon (brainstorming leader)

He studies how people build careers across labor market transitions, including self-employment spells and layoffs, and how these transitions relate to inequality. He leads the brainstorming group on careers, leadership, and inequality.

Josh Feng, assistant professor, University of Utah (brainstorming leader)

He is an applied microeconomist who studies innovation and drug markets. He leads the brainstorming group on the advantages and limitations of Revelio data.

Reuben Hurst, assistant professor, University of Maryland (brainstorming leader)

He studies nonmarket strategy and political dynamics in the workforce. He leads the VRscores project, which merges Revelio profiles with voter files, and leads the brainstorming group on merging Revelio with external data.

How papers were matched to tables

Each paper was matched to the leader whose own research is closest to it. Tables were kept to two or three papers each, following the workshop format.

  • Table 1 pairs two papers on how legal and social pressure affects employee flows with Max Kagan, who links career histories to political records in his own work.
  • Table 2 pairs two papers on entrepreneurial careers and entrepreneurship policy with Grady Raines, who studies entrepreneurship policy with similar research designs.
  • Table 3 pairs two papers on gender, benefits, and career advancement with Beril Yalcinkaya, who studies careers and inequality with LinkedIn-based data.
  • Table 4 pairs two papers on executive mobility and employee reactions to strategic change with Cathy Lu, who studies worker mobility and firm strategy.

Matching used the submitted proposals and survey keywords. CVs were optional in the survey and were not required for matching.
