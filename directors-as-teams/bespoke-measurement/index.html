Bespoke measurement: the evidence, the method, and the framework question

Working memo, 2026-08-31. All rows are verbatim from the v10 coded corpus (1,969 main-specification governance measures across 125 papers).

Definitions

Terms used throughout, each with exactly one meaning:

  • Form: the triple (level, resolution, named_procedure). All three are closed-vocabulary fields already coded on every measure. Two measures have the same form if and only if all three values match exactly. No text is read and no similarity judgment is involved.
    • level: the entity the variable is an attribute of, the smallest one that completes "this variable is an attribute of ___": director, board, committee, CEO, chair, firm, and so on.
    • resolution: what kind of number the construction produces, judged before any monotone transformation (a logged count is still a count): binary, categorical, ordinal, count, share (a part-of-whole ratio), or continuous.
    • named_procedure: the named statistical machinery beyond plain arithmetic, if any: none (levels, tallies, means, shares), concentration index (Blau, Herfindahl), spread statistic, pairwise comparison, graph position (centrality), faultline algorithm, latent weighting (factor/PCA), or model-derived.
  • Sub-construct: the content a measure claims, refined below the coded construct. Financial expertise and technology expertise are different sub-constructs. Measuring different sub-constructs is legitimate specialization, not the bespoke problem.
  • Quantity: the target a measure aims at, given by two coordinates: the entity whose attribute it is (director, CEO, chair, board, firm, industry, country) and the aspect of that entity the number is about, one of:
    • individual status: an attribute of one person (this director is a woman).
    • group composition: the prevalence of an attribute among a group's members: shares, counts, any-member indicators, majority or critical-mass indicators.
    • group heterogeneity: the mixture across three or more categories of an attribute. For a binary attribute, a diversity index such as the Blau index is a deterministic function of the share and is classified as group composition; group heterogeneity is reserved for multi-category attributes.
    • relational: ties, similarity, or comparisons defined against specific other actors.
    • context attribute: a property of an environment rather than of persons or member groups (a country quota law, an industry exposure).
    Director gender and board gender composition are different quantities of the same construct. Measuring different quantities is legitimate specialization, not the bespoke problem.
  • Definition: within a sub-construct and quantity, the raw facts that identify the attribute: what is counted, over whom, with what classifier, against what denominator, over what window. Three studies of financial expertise that use education, a CFO title, and industry tenure are three definitions of one sub-construct. That is the bespoke problem.
  • Raw count: the number of distinct forms (or distinct definitions, or distinct quantities) observed in the corpus for a construct. Each distinct value contributes one to the raw count, whether it is used by one measure or by fifty.
  • Effective number: a raw count can be large solely because many variables are defined in a unique way a single time in the corpus. To show whether a large raw count reflects such singleton usage or disagreement across many papers, we also report the effective number: the inverse Simpson index 1 / Σ pi2, where pi is the share of measures using category i. The effective number equals the raw count when usage is spread evenly and approaches one as usage concentrates in a single category.

The full hierarchy is construct → sub-construct → quantity → definition → form. Apparent measurement heterogeneity within a construct therefore decomposes into three components with different interpretations: variety of quantities (researchers ask different questions about the same construct; legitimate), dispersion of definitions within a quantity (the bespoke problem proper), and dispersion of forms within a quantity and definition (residual inconsistency in how one definition is rendered as a number).

Why form alone is a lower bound

Counting distinct forms within a construct requires no judgment and is computable now. It is a strict lower bound on the number of distinct operationalizations, because it treats everything inside one form cell as identical, however different the underlying definitions are. The following four measures, all from the analytic sample, share the single form director | binary | none and are therefore counted as a single form by the lower bound. Coding pilot

Draft counts from the Dispersion evidence section: gender, a raw count of 18 quantities and 37 definition groups with an effective number of 7.3; expertise, a raw count of 20 quantities and 77 definition groups across 29 sub-constructs with an effective number of 42.7; independence, a raw count of 17 definition groups with an effective number of 3.1; networks, a raw count of 99 groups across 13 sub-constructs with an effective number of 45.7. These figures are a single model's draft. The steps from draft to estimates suitable for publication:

  1. Manual pilot on financial expertise and independence. Financial expertise is the centerpiece (the definition layer does the most work there); independence is the benchmark where we expect low definition dispersion, so it validates the method. Both coauthors independently assign each measure a sub-construct, a quantity, and a definition ID, growing the definition list as they code.
  2. Adjudication becomes rules. Compute agreement, adjudicate disagreements, and write each resolution into an explicit rule. The rule set becomes a versioned coding prompt, the same loop used for the amount/structure classification.
  3. LLM extension by assignment, not pairwise comparison. Pairwise same/different judgments are quadratic in cost and not transitive, so they never yield a partition. Instead the model assigns each remaining record to the adjudicated definition library or proposes a new entry; two independent runs, disagreements adjudicated, accuracy validated on a held-out slice of the manual pilot before any unpiloted construct is trusted.

Edge cases the pilot must settle by explicit rule

  • Binary attributes and diversity indices. Settled: for a binary attribute, a Blau or entropy index is a deterministic function of the share and is classified as group composition; group heterogeneity is reserved for multi-category attributes. Where an index mixes gender with other traits, the record is classified by what the measure does and flagged.
  • Polarity. Fraction insiders and fraction independent carry the same information with opposite sign: one definition plus a polarity flag, or the counts double-count complements.
  • Thresholds. A majority-independent dummy and the independent share are one definition in two forms. Threshold values live only in the operationalization prose (about 460 records carry threshold language), so the pilot extracts them as a column.
  • Data source. The same classifier concept from BoardEx versus hand-coded proxy statements is one definition with two implementations; report implementation dispersion separately. The data_source field is unnormalized free text (1,616 distinct strings) and needs a cleanup pass first.
  • Claimed lineage. Measures introduced as "following X et al." sometimes deviate from X; identical constructions sometimes cite nothing. Code lineage as its own column. Whether a citation pins down a construction is a publishable side observation.
  • Composites. Faultlines and indices built from named constituents are excluded from the pilot; their dispersion is a property of their constituents.
  • Indeterminate records. Operationalizations the source text does not fully state get an explicit indeterminate code and a reported share. No reconstruction.

The two-problem framing

The corpus supports the proposed motivation structure:

  1. The composition-model problem (structural): the field's measures aggregate individual attributes and discard configuration. This is the manuscript's existing Section 5 evidence: 88% of measures are amounts, 12% structural, 19% relational, 34 of 1,969 compare directors within the same board.
  2. The bespoke-operationalization problem (consistency): even within that architecture, measures are not standardized across papers. This is the Dispersion evidence section, and it compounds the structural problem: results built on different definitions do not accumulate.

A new subsection presenting the dispersion table and a subset of the definition-level evidence would follow the manuscript's Section 5.3 and would provide the transition into Section 6 and the companion paper.

A terminology decision before either paper goes out. "Compilational rather than compositional" uses the Kozlowski and Klein multilevel vocabulary, where composition means isomorphic aggregation and compilation means configural emergence. The companion paper's core tool is compositional data analysis, and it represents boards as compositions on a simplex. If the motivation names composition as the problem and the proposed solution is then presented under the name compositional data analysis, readers are likely to conflate the two senses. Options: use "configural" or "relational" for the fix and avoid the Kozlowski-Klein term, or spend a footnote separating the two senses explicitly.

The framework's board-level object is itself an aggregation. It preserves the mixture of director types, not the pairwise configuration among directors. The claim the paper can support is that the director-level representation is retained underneath, so who differs from whom is recoverable at the director grain, and the contextual layer carries the configural information. "We fix the structural problem" without that qualification overclaims.

The architecture claim and the risk of standard proliferation

The relevant failure mode is standard proliferation: a framework introduced to consolidate fifteen existing standards becomes a sixteenth. The question-specific-measures framing is the right defense. Those question-specific measures (financial expertise and restatements, technology expertise and patenting, gender diversity and performance) are built from the attribute matrix and its vocabularies, not from the fitted K=12 types. The release artifact should be the pipeline, the director-year attribute matrix, and the documented defaults, with the fitted types as one product of it. Three supporting points:

  • Researcher degrees of freedom. The vocabulary maps from raw biography text to categories are unavoidable choices. Every bespoke paper already makes exactly these choices, privately and unreported; the expertise listing shows each paper's implicit classifier. The framework makes the same choices once, in public, with a changelog. The claim is "auditable and common across users", never "choice-free".
  • Standards win on switching costs. Open data and method are necessary, not sufficient. Adoption comes from a board-year panel that merges on a standard firm key more easily than rebuilding a measure from ISS. The G-index achieved broad adoption in part because it was distributed as a ready-to-merge dataset.
  • Scope the claim. The framework competes where bespoke proliferation is least defensible: describing and controlling for board composition as a whole. It does not displace construct-specific hypothesis tests.

NMF versus similarity networks

Both approaches answer the same question: given a large set of attributes describing individual directors, how do we reduce those attributes to a small number of director types? Both begin from exactly the same object, the director-year attribute matrix. They differ in which side of that matrix they collapse.

Note on scope: this concerns attribute similarity networks, in which directors are tied by resembling each other. It is distinct from interlock networks, in which directors are tied by shared board service. Interlocks measure relational position rather than intrinsic attributes and, in the current architecture, belong to the contextual layer; they do not compete with NMF.

1 · From one primitive to two different objects

Shared primitive

The director-year attribute matrix. One row per director per year (251,345 rows in the current panel); one column per binary attribute (82 columns across roles, skills, sector experience, career stage, and education and credentials).

▼
Non-negative matrix factorization Collapse the columns: summarize the attributes.
Step 1 — Factor

Approximate the matrix as the product of two smaller non-negative tables, chosen to reproduce the original as closely as possible. The number of types, K, is set by the researcher. Non-negativity means an attribute can only ever add to a type, never cancel against another.

▼
Output A — Type profiles

K rows, one per type, each a set of non-negative weights over the 82 named attributes. This is what makes a type sayable: a type loading heavily on CFO role, CPA credential and finance skill is the financial-expert type.

▼
Output B — Director loadings

One row per director-year: K non-negative weights, normalized to sum to one. A director who is 0.6 financial expert and 0.4 academic advisor is recorded as exactly that.

▼
Board level

Average the loading rows of the directors sitting on a board. The result is another K-vector of the same kind, so a director, a board, a board's own past and the panel average are all comparable with one metric.

The attribute columns are never discarded, so every object downstream can be read back in attribute terms.

Similarity network Collapse the rows: summarize the directors.
Step 1 — Pairwise similarity

Compare every pair of director rows with a similarity measure — Jaccard (shared attributes over total attributes) for binary data, or cosine. The output is a square director-by-director similarity matrix.

The 82 attribute columns are gone at this step. From here on the object is a relation among directors, not a description of them.

▼
Step 2 — Sparsify into a graph

A dense square matrix is not yet a network. Keep only strong edges: a similarity threshold, or each director's k nearest neighbours. This step is unavoidable at panel scale, and the cut-off determines which communities appear.

▼
Step 3 — Partition

Community detection (Louvain, Leiden) searches for the division of the graph that maximizes modularity: within-community edge weight in excess of what a degree-matched random graph would produce. Each director lands in exactly one community.

▼
Output — Communities as types

One label per director. The type has no profile; it is described afterwards, by returning to the attribute matrix and tabulating what its members happen to have in common — a description the type was not defined by.

▼
Board level

A count of directors per community: a board of nine is three of type A, two of type B, and so on. Integer-valued, with structural zeros in most cells.

Variants replace the partition step with a spectral embedding or node2vec coordinates. Those return soft coordinates but forfeit the community labels, and they do not aggregate additively to the board.

The asymmetry. NMF yields the similarity network as a by-product: the similarity between two directors is the distance between their loading rows, a comparison in K type dimensions rather than 82 attribute dimensions. A similarity network cannot hand you the type profiles, because the information needed to write them down was discarded at its first step.

3 · Which approach preserves relational structure

This is the dimension on which similarity networks are most competitive, and it is the substantive question behind the choice. A similarity network makes the pairwise comparison its primary object: director to director, who differs from whom, at the dyad. NMF followed by averaging to a board mean is itself an aggregation, and an average discards the configuration among the directors it averages over.

NMF retains the director-level representation underneath the board mean, so the dyadic comparison is recoverable at the director grain, and the contextual layer carries the configural information. The similarity network yields the dyadic object and nothing else; NMF yields the dyadic object, named types, and a board-level measure in the same space. NMF wins the dimension, but on the strength of the director-level representation rather than the board mean. What needs qualifying in the paper is therefore not the choice of estimator but the phrase “we fix the structural problem”: the board-level object is an aggregation, and should be described as one.

4 · Common ground and a comparison appendix

These are not rival paradigms. Symmetric NMF of a director similarity matrix is a relaxation of spectral clustering, and NMF of the attribute matrix is a soft clustering of it; community detection is approximately the hard-assignment limit of a closely related factorization.

The proposal for the call is a comparison appendix: fit both, report where community assignments agree with dominant NMF types, and report the disagreements. It is cheap, it gives the discussion a concrete object instead of a framework argument, it tests the one substantive objection (whether the types share parts), and it lets the paper claim robustness to the choice rather than defend it.
